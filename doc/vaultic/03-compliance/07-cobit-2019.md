# COBIT 2019 Governance and Management Assessment: Vaultic & VaulticDB

[← Back to compliance index](00-overview.md)

## 1. Executive Summary

This document assesses how **vaultic** and **`vaulticdb`** support the
**COBIT 2019** governance and management system for enterprise information and
technology (I&T). COBIT governs organizations and their management systems; it
does not certify backup products. Vaultic is an enabling technology and evidence
source for selected governance and management objectives, especially risk,
security, availability, continuity, configuration, operations, service
providers, data, monitoring, and compliance.

The assessment distinguishes:

- **Direct enablement:** vaultic implements a control activity or generates
  primary evidence.
- **Shared enablement:** vaultic supplies data/capability, while management owns
  process design, accountability, approval, and review.
- **Organizational:** the objective is predominantly outside product scope.

COBIT performance and capability ratings belong to the operator's processes,
not to vaultic. The scores below measure only product enablement.

### Domain Assessment Matrix

| COBIT domain | Vaultic contribution | Enablement score | Assessment |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **EDM — Evaluate, Direct and Monitor** | Risk, value, resource, and assurance evidence | **3.3 / 5.0** | Supporting evidence; governance decisions remain board/management-owned |
| **APO — Align, Plan and Organize** | Architecture, risk, security, data, supplier, continuity inputs | **4.0 / 5.0** | Substantial technical input |
| **BAI — Build, Acquire and Implement** | Controlled change, configuration/schema integrity, release rollback | **3.8 / 5.0** | Shared |
| **DSS — Deliver, Service and Support** | Operations, incidents, continuity, security services, process controls | **4.5 / 5.0** | Strongest management domain |
| **MEA — Monitor, Evaluate and Assess** | Checks, metrics, audit evidence, compliance mappings | **4.2 / 5.0** | Strong evidence; assurance independence remains external |

**Overall COBIT 2019 Enablement Rating:** **3.96 / 5.0 (Substantial technical
enablement; enterprise governance, benefits ownership, budgeting, workforce,
internal audit, and formal process capability remain organizational).**

## 2. Governance-System Scope and Responsibility

Vaultic should appear in an enterprise COBIT design as:

1. A **service component** of backup, archival, restore, and cyber-recovery.
2. An **information asset processor** that protects repository data and metadata.
3. A **supplier/software dependency** with Go, Rust, SlateDB, cloud SDK, and
   storage-provider dependencies.
4. A **control evidence source** for availability, integrity, placement,
   retention, ownership, restore testing, and incident reconstruction.

Vaultic is not the governance system. It does not assign accountable executives,
approve investment, own business outcomes, conduct independent audit, manage
workforce performance, or determine regulatory applicability.

## 3. EDM — Evaluate, Direct and Monitor

### EDM01 — Ensured Governance Framework Setting and Maintenance

**Enablement: Supporting.** The
[design and planning documentation](../README.md) provides a traceable chain from
strategic objectives to architecture, compliance assessments, tactical phases,
tests, constraints, and rollback. Management must incorporate that evidence into
its own governance framework, decision rights, and policy hierarchy.

### EDM02 — Ensured Benefits Delivery

**Enablement: Supporting.** Analytics expose growth, churn, ownership, storage
placement, backend cost attributes, and archive promotion economics. The
[competitive analysis](../01-strategy/02-competitive-analysis.md) supplies TCO
and lock-in hypotheses. Management must validate assumptions, assign benefit
owners, and measure realized savings/recovery outcomes.

### EDM03 — Ensured Risk Optimization

**Enablement: Substantial.** Explicit unknown states, placement durability,
offsite deadlines, WORM-aware deletion, known constraints, and failure-mode
specific tests give management concrete risk indicators. Vaultic does not set
risk appetite or accept residual risk.

### EDM04 — Ensured Resource Optimization

**Enablement: Supporting.** Capacity estimates, pack sizing, cache limits,
bandwidth/request-rate metadata, analytics storage forecasts, and workload
priorities support resource decisions. Budget, staffing, sourcing, and capacity
approval remain organizational.

### EDM05 — Ensured Stakeholder Engagement

**Enablement: Limited.** Stable JSON reports, compliance mappings, and audit
records support stakeholder reporting. Stakeholder identification,
communications, escalation, and disclosure remain management responsibilities.

## 4. APO — Align, Plan and Organize

### APO01 — Managed I&T Management Framework

Vaultic supplies documented lifecycle, ownership boundaries, implementation
conventions, and phase exit criteria. The operator must integrate them into its
I&T policies, process catalog, RACI, exceptions, and assurance calendar.

### APO02 — Managed Strategy

The standard Restic/Rustic format, vaulticdb scale architecture, open recovery
path, and multi-provider roadmap support strategies that prioritize portability,
resilience, and low lock-in. Strategic alignment and portfolio prioritization
remain executive decisions.

### APO03 — Managed Enterprise Architecture

**Strong enablement.** Process boundaries, protocol, engine resolution, binary
schema, legacy interop, placement model, analytics, and phased migrations are
specified in [Architecture](../02-architecture/00-overview.md). Operators must
add deployment-specific network, identity, host, CMDB, and business-service
views.

### APO06 — Managed Budget and Costs

Backend price, capacity, request, egress, minimum-retention, object-overhead, and
pack-size data support cost modeling. Vaultic does not perform enterprise
budgeting or financial approval.

### APO09 — Managed Service Agreements

Placement status, offsite deadlines, retention state, check results, catch-up
lag, and recovery tests can become measurable SLO evidence. Contracts, SLAs,
service credits, and business-service definitions remain external.

### APO10 — Managed Vendors

Backend/provider inventory, failure domains, storage classes, and standard-format
portability support supplier assessment and exit planning. Due diligence,
contract review, concentration limits, jurisdiction analysis, and annual vendor
review remain operator-owned.

### APO11 — Managed Quality

Schema validation, focused and broad tests, race tests, differential checks,
benchmark gates, and explicit exit criteria support quality management. An
organizational quality-management system and independent quality assurance are
not provided.

### APO12 — Managed Risk

**Strong enablement.** Relevant evidence includes:

- [Known Constraints](../04-roadmap/04-known-constraints.md).
- Placement durability and offsite RPO status.
- Crawl debt, unresolved references, malformed records, and explicit unknowns.
- Integrity and restore-test results.
- Rollout/rollback controls and experimental feature status.

Risk ownership, likelihood/impact scoring, treatment approval, and acceptance
remain external.

### APO13 — Managed Security

**Strong enablement.** Client-side encryption, secret vaulting, authenticated
transport, least-exposure defaults, WORM retention, lifecycle logging, SIEM
routing, integrity checks, and repair/recovery support the security-management
system. Host hardening, SOC operations, IAM/RBAC, MFA, and security policy remain
operator responsibilities.

### APO14 — Managed Data

**Strong enablement.** Vaultic manages backup data lineage, immutable revisions,
ownership attributes, topology/path classifications, retention and residency,
reference counts, GDPR scope, and erasure evidence. Enterprise data ownership,
classification taxonomy, quality rules, and legal-use decisions remain
external.

## 5. BAI — Build, Acquire and Implement

### BAI02 / BAI03 — Requirements and Solution Identification/Build

The roadmap links objectives to architecture, implementation steps, tests, and
exit criteria. Requirements traceability is strong within the documented
vaultic scope; business case approval and full enterprise requirements remain
external.

### BAI06 — Managed I&T Changes

- Risky changes progress through Alpha → Beta → Stable states.
- Schema/protocol versions are explicit and incompatible state fails closed.
- [Rollout and Rollback](../04-roadmap/03-rollout-and-rollback.md) documents
  reversal paths.
- Migration phases preserve legacy JSON indexes until parity is verified.

Formal change advisory, segregation of duties, emergency-change review, and
enterprise release calendars remain operator-owned.

### BAI07 — Managed I&T Change Acceptance and Transitioning

Phase-specific unit, integration, scale, safety, and compatibility tests provide
acceptance evidence. Operators must add deployment-specific acceptance criteria,
restore drills, performance baselines, and business-owner signoff.

### BAI09 — Managed Assets

Backend, pack, placement, repository, and build identities provide technical
asset records. These should be reconciled into the enterprise CMDB and software
asset inventory.

### BAI10 — Managed Configuration

Repository configuration, daemon capabilities, schema version, manifest,
backend registry, placement policy, and analytics classification config are
versioned and validated. Host, network, cloud-IAM, and SIEM configuration remain
outside vaultic and need separate baseline control.

### BAI11 — Managed Projects

The tactical phase roadmap supplies sequencing, dependencies, tests, and exit
criteria, but it is not a full project-management system. Funding, staffing,
schedule, issue management, and stakeholder reporting remain organizational.

## 6. DSS — Deliver, Service and Support

### DSS01 — Managed Operations — Rating: 4.5 / 5.0

- Singleton daemon lifecycle, health/capability RPCs, bounded scans/batches,
  backpressure, deadlines, and transactional durability support stable
  operations.
- Placement scheduler, deletion queue, promotion/repack, history rollups,
  analytics lifecycle, checks, and export/rebuild commands are
  operator-controlled workflows.
- Metrics and categorized logs support runbooks and monitoring.

Operators must supply job scheduling, on-call coverage, runbooks, environment
monitoring, and service desk integration.

### DSS02 — Managed Service Requests and Incidents — Rating: 3.8 / 5.0

Vaultic supplies diagnostics, stable JSON, event categories, repair commands,
and recovery mechanisms. Ticket intake, severity, assignment, escalation,
communications, and closure are external service-management processes.

### DSS03 — Managed Problems — Rating: 4.0 / 5.0

Append-only event history, immutable revisions, crawl debt, placement lineage,
check findings, and explicit errors support root-cause analysis. The organization
must maintain known-error records, corrective actions, trend reviews, and
problem ownership.

### DSS04 — Managed Continuity — Rating: 5.0 / 5.0

- Open Restic/Rustic format provides an independent recovery toolchain.
- Legacy JSON projection protects against loss of SlateDB/vaulticdb state.
- Hot/cold and multi-provider placement protect against infrastructure failure.
- Warm-up, resumable/selective restore, and full verification support recovery
  at cold-storage scale.
- Placement durability and offsite deadlines make resilience measurable.

Business impact analysis, RTO/RPO approval, alternate facilities, crisis
communications, and exercise governance remain operator-owned.

### DSS05 — Managed Security Services — Rating: 4.4 / 5.0

Encryption, managed-secret retrieval, authenticated transport, WORM retention,
integrity monitoring, SIEM export, and explicit unknown-state handling provide
strong security-service controls. Identity governance, SOC staffing, EDR/NDR,
vulnerability scanning, threat intelligence, and MFA remain external.

### DSS06 — Managed Business Process Controls — Rating: 4.0 / 5.0

Transactional publication, immutable revisions, reference counts, retention
checks, durability predicates, idempotency, dry runs, confirmation gates, and
stable reports enforce correctness within backup processes. Business process
owners must define authorization, reconciliation frequency, exception handling,
and evidence review.

## 7. MEA — Monitor, Evaluate and Assess

### MEA01 — Managed Performance and Conformance Monitoring — Rating: 4.4 / 5.0

Vaultic exposes capacity, placement, tier, history, growth, ownership, lag,
cache, unresolved-state, and integrity metrics. Benchmark profiles define
quantitative gates. Operators must set target thresholds, retain trends, assign
owners, and conduct management review.

### MEA02 — Managed System of Internal Control — Rating: 4.1 / 5.0

Checks and rebuild comparisons can test whether metadata, placement, retention,
and recovery controls operate as designed. This document and the
[NIST CSF](05-nist-csf-2.0.md), [CIS Controls](06-cis-controls-v8.md),
[NIS2](03-nis2.md), [ISO 27001](02-iso27001.md), and [GDPR](01-gdpr.md)
assessments provide control mappings. Control ownership, deficiency grading,
remediation tracking, and independent review remain organizational.

### MEA03 — Managed Compliance With External Requirements — Rating: 4.0 / 5.0

Stable evidence, retention history, key-access logs, integrity results,
placement records, GDPR audit/erasure analysis, and compliance mappings support
legal and regulatory assessment. Determining applicability, interpreting law,
filing notifications, and obtaining legal assurance remain outside vaultic.

### MEA04 — Managed Assurance — Rating: 3.2 / 5.0

Vaultic can provide evidence to assurance providers but cannot independently
assure itself. Operators should preserve immutable evidence, define audit scope,
provide reproducible test procedures, and commission independent technical and
process assurance where required.

## 8. COBIT Design-Factor Guidance

When incorporating vaultic into a COBIT 2019 governance system, adjust the
priority of objectives using deployment-specific design factors:

| Design factor | Vaultic implication |
|---|---|
| Enterprise strategy | Cost leadership favors open format and object-storage economics; innovation favors analytics and automation |
| Enterprise goals | Resilience and privacy increase priority of APO12/13/14, DSS04/05/06, MEA02/03 |
| Risk profile | Ransomware, provider failure, cold-retrieval delay, credential compromise, and metadata loss drive placement and exercise requirements |
| I&T-related issues | Legacy JSON scale limits and proprietary lock-in justify vaulticdb and deterministic export |
| Threat landscape | High threat raises verification frequency, immutable retention, offsite copies, and SOC integration |
| Compliance requirements | GDPR, NIS2, ISO 27001, sector regulation, and retention law determine evidence and deletion policy |
| Role of I&T | Strategic backup/recovery requires stronger governance and independent assurance than a utility archive |
| Sourcing model | Multi-cloud/NFS sourcing raises APO10, DSS01, and concentration-risk requirements |
| Implementation methods | Feature flags and phased migration support controlled agile delivery without abandoning compatibility |
| Technology adoption | Experimental phases remain opt-in until benchmark and safety gates pass |

## 9. Recommended COBIT Adoption Actions

1. **Assign accountability:** Name accountable owners for backup service,
   security, data, continuity, provider management, and control assurance.
2. **Register the service:** Model vaultic repositories, vaulticdb instances,
   source filesystems, cloud providers, credentials, and recovery dependencies
   in the service catalog and CMDB.
3. **Define service objectives:** Approve RTO, RPO/offsite deadline, durability,
   retention, verification cadence, restore-test frequency, and evidence
   retention.
4. **Build process controls:** Require reviewed configuration, dry-run/approval
   for destructive operations, separation of backup and storage credentials,
   periodic reconciliation, and exception handling.
5. **Monitor outcomes:** Feed placement, integrity, lag, capacity, restore, and
   SIEM evidence into MEA01 dashboards and management review.
6. **Close known gaps:** Add SBOM/SCA, vulnerability disclosure/intake,
   privileged-access MFA/RBAC, independent penetration testing, and assurance
   review to the enterprise control plan.
7. **Exercise and improve:** Run recovery and incident exercises, document
   lessons learned, update risk treatment, and feed improvements back into the
   roadmap and operating procedures.
